        ## QCD Matter at Extreme Conditions

        My research lies in theoretical and phenomenological high-energy nuclear physics. Quantum chromodynamics (QCD) predicts that, at sufficiently high temperature and energy density, quarks and gluons deconfine into a quark–gluon plasma (QGP) — a state of matter that filled the early Universe microseconds after the Big Bang and is recreated, briefly, in high-energy nuclear collisions. My work asks how this medium forms, how it evolves, and what it reveals about QCD in a regime where the usual perturbative tools break down.

        ## Relativistic Nuclear Collisions

        Experimentally this medium is studied across a range of collision systems: large heavy-ion collisions (Pb+Pb, Au+Au), light-ion collisions (O+O), and small, asymmetric systems (p+Pb, d+Au). Moving from large to small systems raises a question I work on directly — how far toward smaller collision systems can QGP-like final-state effects persist, and where does the picture change? This isn't a settled question, and part of the work is being explicit about what current data can and can't distinguish.

        ## Heavy Flavor as a Probe

        Heavy charm and bottom quarks are produced early in a collision, before the medium forms, and then propagate through it — which makes them a useful probe of the medium's properties. Much of my work focuses specifically on bottomonium and charmonium (quarkonium): bound states of a heavy quark and its antiquark whose suppression and regeneration in the medium carry information about its temperature, density, and evolution.

        ## Cold and Hot Nuclear Matter

        A modification seen in the data can come from two different places: cold nuclear matter (CNM) effects present even without a QGP (nuclear parton distributions, coherent energy loss, momentum broadening), or hot-medium interactions once a QGP actually forms. Separating these two contributions, rather than attributing everything to the hot medium, is central to how I approach this work.

        ## Transport

        Methodologically, I connect QCD theory to measurements at RHIC and the LHC using open-quantum-system transport (Lindblad-equation evolution), semiclassical kinetic transport, and hydrodynamic medium backgrounds. These are tools in service of the physics questions above, not the point of the work on their own.

        See [Current Projects](/projects/) for what I'm actively working on and [Publications](/publications/) for the resulting papers. I collaborate through the [HEFTY Topical Collaboration](https://hefty.tamu.edu/) on Heavy-Flavor Theory for QCD Matter.
