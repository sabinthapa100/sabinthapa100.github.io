> Have you ever wondered what the hottest matter in the universe is? It's the primordial matter of the Big Bang, and we can recreate it right here on Earth. By smashing atomic nuclei together at nearly the speed of light, we produce temperatures exceeding 1,000,000,000,000 (a trillion) Kelvin—roughly 100,000 times hotter than the core of the Sun. At these extremes, the boundaries of fundamental particles melt away, freeing quarks and gluons to form a perfect, frictionless fluid known as Quark-Gluon Plasma (QGP).

I study quarkonium production and transport in relativistic nuclear collisions, connecting quantum and semiclassical transport calculations with measurements at RHIC and the LHC. The work spans bottomonium and charmonium production across small, light-ion, and heavy-ion collision systems, including cold- and hot-nuclear-matter effects.

**Research**

- QCD matter at extreme conditions and the quark-gluon plasma
- Relativistic nuclear collisions, from small and light-ion to heavy-ion systems
- Heavy flavor and quarkonium as probes of cold and hot nuclear matter
- Open-quantum-system transport, semiclassical transport, hydrodynamics, and scientific computing

**Exploring and learning**

AI for Physics and AI for Science, AI Safety, quantum computing, and radiation physics are broader interests and learning areas, not established specialties.
