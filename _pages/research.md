## QCD Matter at Extreme Conditions

My research lies in theoretical and phenomenological high-energy nuclear physics. Quantum chromodynamics (QCD) predicts that, at sufficiently high temperature and energy density, quarks and gluons deconfine into a quark-gluon plasma (QGP), a state of matter that filled the early Universe and is recreated briefly in high-energy nuclear collisions.

## Relativistic Nuclear Collisions

I study quarkonium (both bottomonium and charmonium) production and transport across small, light-ion, and heavy-ion collision systems. Moving from large systems such as Pb+Pb and Au+Au to O+O and p+Pb raises the question of how far QGP-like final-state effects persist as collision systems become smaller.

## Heavy Flavor as a Probe

Charm and bottom quarks are produced early in collisions and then propagate through the evolving medium. Their bound states, quarkonia, provide probes of the temperature, density, and evolution of the medium.

## Cold and Hot Nuclear Matter

Observed modifications can arise from cold nuclear matter (CNM) effects present before a QGP forms, or from interactions with the hot medium. Distinguishing these contributions is central to interpreting quarkonium measurements.

## Methods

I connect QCD theory to measurements at RHIC and the LHC using hydrodynamic backgrounds, open-quantum-system transport, semiclassical kinetic transport, and scientific computing. These methods support the physics questions rather than define them.
